Moll de l’Ós

8 rue des Beaux-Arts, Paris

From May 27 to June 26 2026

Alzueta Gallery Paris was pleased to present Moll de l’os, an exhibition of new works by Claudia Valsells.

‘Moll de l’os brings together a body of paintings made throughout 2026 that investigate the capacity of color to hold experience. With no predetermined subject matter and no representational intent, the works emerge as records of a process sustained over time — one in which painting has become a space of attention and transformation. In these mid-scale formats, the pictorial matter concentrates gesture, silence and presence, inviting the viewer into a direct encounter with what is essential. These are emotional registers, sediments of a lived process. For me, painting is a way of drawing closer to the core — to the marrow of each piece. It is how I test the possibility of continuing to paint, even when it is difficult.

With Moll de l’os, I point to the place where artistic practice confronts its own nucleus — where colour does not explain: it insists.

My hope is that the viewer, too, might connect with their own marrow, and find a personal experience that reaches beyond a purely visual reading.’

Claudia Valsells

‘Míra’m no deixis de mirar-me’

Solo show at Alzueta Gallery,
Palau de Casavells

From April to June 2025

‘Mira’m, no deixis de mirar-me’
‘Look at me, don’t stop looking at me’invites us to explore the work and career of Claudia Valsells, where color is both a physical substance and an intangible emotion. Throughout a life devoted to researching and experimenting with color and the creation of her own palette, the artist has created a language to channel her experience and perception of the world. In her work, color goes beyond aesthetics to become a meaningful, almost linguistic, element.

For the artist, materializing a color is a complex process in three acts: first, she envisions it; then, she creates it through a unique combination of pigments; and finally, she applies it to the canvas, where it engages in dialogue with other colors. Valsells builds on technical knowledge but also surrenders to intuition, finding in painting a space of refuge and communication—both with herself and with the viewer. Each piece, each chromatic interaction, emerges from a delicate interplay of prior knowledge, instinct, and the artist’s lived experience.

The exhibition brings together, for the first time, the series What’s Color?, Color Dialogues, The Joy of Painting, Transitions, and her most recent work, Mapa, in which Valsells explores chromatic juxtaposition with a newfound maturity. This collection of works showcases the evolution of her practice while highlighting the importance of the viewer’s gaze in art. The title, ‘Mira’m, no deixis de mirar-me’, refers, on one hand, to the act of exposing oneself to public recognition and, on the other, to the essential need to open up to others, not only in the artistic context but in all aspects of life.

The concepts of the eye and the gaze have often been understood as symbols of otherness. In this sense, Sartre asserted that it is through the gaze of others that we recognize ourselves. This dimension of the other is what constitutes and defines us, allowing us to exist as subjects. According to the French philosopher, it is also what humanizes us and, therefore, enables communication. Daily experiences and interactions with others shape multiple identities: we are not made of a single piece.

Valsells’ work becomes a space for connection, where the viewer and the artwork meet through the experience of color. In this exchange, the work finds meaning, and we, as viewers, also uncover something about ourselves. ‘Mira’m, no deixis de mirar-me’ is an open invitation to this silent dialogue, where color becomes a language, speaking to us about who we are, about others, and about the artist’s own journey.

What’s Color III

Terranova Bookstore

April 2024

‘What’s Color?’ is a personal project I started in 2020 where I assign a color to people who inspire and admire me and whom I ask about their personal relationship with color.
I presented this third edition at Llibreria Terranova in Barcelona during April 2024.

During the past months I have invited them to consider and answer the question: ‘What does color mean to you?’

First of all, to open up a dialogue, to grasp new ways of seeing colors, other perspectives, but also to better understand the world. I wanted to think of color in a broader context, outside of the artistic view, and to discover the notion of color as it is thought of by others.

Now, this whole journey has become a process of identifying and synthesizing knowledge, when possible, into color, with the wish to create and share a ‘Color Glossary’ where color meets perception. For what purpose?

Because to me, COLOR is a universal visual emotion, capable of reaching and inspiring every human being.

Transitions

Solo show at Alzueta Gallery,
Barcelona

September 2023

Transitions is a reflection of the constant duality between the creative process and the finished work. In essence, the work itself becomes a field of oscillation, and it is within this oscillation that it finds its meaning and purpose.

The colors, carefully interwoven , interact with white, gradually seeking juxtaposition. It is within this juxtaposition that we find the transition, which in this context can be understood as a change, a movement, a flow of energy, a recycling of the same. This transition is not limited solely to a formal change but transcends to a deeper level where the works resonate with each other, creating a visual symphony that evolves as we move through the space, crossing invisible lines of connection that keep the canvases in constant conversation.

This exhibition marks a transition from the initial chromatic verticality to a broader and more diverse palette; genuine and light. It is a testimony to how I try to evolved over time and a testament to this quest for new horizons.

This time, under a crucial motto: the work is the path to color. Color exists before the work takes shape and gradually reveals itself as we progress in the creative process. The work is built from the inside out, as an ever-developing process enriched with every brushstroke and every decision made in the studio.

I am not just talking about a mixture of colors and forms. It is a source of energy, a manifestation of the deep connection.

‘To rethinking, oscillating between the process of the artwork and the artwork itself. As a field of oscillation, without oscillation, there is no artwork. Its process and its culmination. Like the reality that surrounds us, ever-changing as well. The intertwined colors interact with white, seeking juxtaposition, the transition, understood as a change, understood as a movement. Transition from chromatic verticality to the color palette, always expanding. The artwork is not the color, the artwork is a path to color. Color exists before the artwork does, the artwork will come with time built from the inside out. The artwork, as something more, is ENERGY.’

Claudia Valsells

What’s Color II

Espacio Pavilon
Madrid

December 2021

‘The memory of one of my architecture teachers taking off one of his reddish-brown English shoes, rolling up the tour of his pants to show the navy blue color of his socks, and cutting out a piece of clothing from the striped Oxford shirt.All this spectacle in the middle of a construction site to show respectively the color of the wood, the color of the carpet and the different shades of the walls, is an image that I remember with some nostalgia, but that cannot be repeated today.’

Text commissioned by the architect Cristian Cirici on the occasion of my first color chart self edited in 2000.

From the scientific experiment to the artistic concern, from Munsell’s diagrams and Goethe’s studies to Le Corbusier’s Salubra and the almost infinite possible combinations of electronics, the color chart has always existed to facilitate the communication of color.
A mere organic, hierarchical and chromatic division in its origin, a method of plastic expression and aesthetic positioning in its development, this instrument, which came to democratize design and encourage the creative impulse, has seen in its years of life how the use of color by the architectural sector has been diminishing, limited perhaps because of certain social prejudices and canons encouraged by advertising and the market. The pristine white, the sad black, the nondescript gray, the garish red.

Monopolized the business of colors available by the painting companies, relegated the work of the colorist to the background due to ignorance or fear of architects and clients to make a mistake or be branded as eccentric; it is in this context that the artist Claudia Valsells had an idea: to create a specific chromatic selection for this sector.

In this exhibition we wanted to highlight the work of the colorist, the process of selecting and prescribing colors for a project and the color chart as a tool that facilitates and enables communication between architects and designers.
For this reason we have compiled in the exhibition a series of work tools that Claudia, an artist linked to color since her beginnings as a mural painter, used for the elaboration of her own chart: notebooks, mixers, spatulas, annotations, paint covers. It is therefore a retrospective, documentary and personal exhibition, where experiences, stories and testimonies are mixed.

The result of more than twenty years of work by the artist, collaborating with architects and designers in the definition of colors, this color chart is composed of 142 colors applied with real paint, which allows to see the exact reproduction of the color as it will look once applied on the surface. This range of colors, developed for use in architecture, both exterior and interior, allows the colorist to demonstrate Valsells’ natural ability and chromatic instinct, the knowledge of the language that color implies.

This color chart, and the work of its author, Claudia Valsells, is an invitation to explore, to play, and to dare to prescribe colors. Any color from this palette can be achieved, and it is worth the effort to see exactly what one is looking for. We want to reopen the dialogue on the use of color in architecture, to offer one more tool, among many others, that encourages that return to color, with knowledge, dialogue and vision.

Shift towards coincidence

Group exhibition at Alzueta Gallery,
Palau de Casavells

June to July 2026

Aythamy Armas, Manolo Ballesteros, Berni Puig, Enrich.R, Claudia Valsells, Alexander Wertheim, Maria Yelletisch.

Pictorial abstraction has shifted over time within the history of art, moving from the avant-gardes of the 20th century to a broad and heterogeneous contemporary territory. More than a style, it has become a way of thinking painting: a field where the image is no longer dependent on representation but is activated through gesture, color, matter, and rhythm.

In this shift, painting has steadily affirmed its autonomy. From early breaks with figuration to contemporary practices, abstraction has opened a field in which the pictorial surface becomes a site of experience, rather than a window onto an external world. In this sense, when Piet Mondrian stated that ‘abstract art is not the creation of another reality but the true version of reality,’ he was not introducing an opposition between worlds, but rather a way of situating painting within a more essential experience, freed from the need to describe the visible.

The works brought together in this exhibition participate in this expanded field. In them, painting is not fixed as a final image but unfolds as process, accumulation, or tension. Color acts as force, gesture as impulse, repetition as latent structure. Each artist articulates a different way of inhabiting this condition: from chromatic vibration, systemic construction, accumulated matter, or intuitive order.

For Claudia Valsells (Barcelona, Spain, 1969), color is the core of her practice, understood as both structural and sensorial force. Her works arise from chromatic relationships in which intuition, action, and technical knowledge converge. In these subtle modulations, color becomes a presence that alters and guides perception.

Marco, Límite y Lugar

Group exhibition
Alzueta Gallery, Madrid

March 2026

Jordi Alcaraz, Aythamy Armas, Max Cobalto, Enrich R, Iván Franco, Nuria Maria, Bruno Ollé, Maru Quiñonero, Guim Tió, Claudia Valsells, Maria Yelletisch

For this exhibition, Miquel Alzueta, founder of the gallery, has selected and loaned eleven 16th- and 17th-century Italian frames from his private collection, inviting each artist to illuminate one of these frames with their work. This process subverts the usual approach, where a frame is chosen to fit a piece; here, the artwork is instead created to adapt to the frame’s dimensions.

Beyond being mere ornamentation, the frame defines the boundary and space of the artwork, becoming a place that not only encloses but actively participates in the composition. The exhibition presents the works separate from their traditional frames, prompting viewers to reconsider the frame as an independent entity—one as significant as the painting itself in shaping the interpretation of the work within the exhibition space.

Between the almost four centuries that separate the oldest frame from the most recent painting, that both coexist and bring together ice and fire, not as opposing elements but as complementary ones: both necessary, both desirous of each other, coinciding for a time in a shared reality. Like love and desire.

Here, paintings and frames nourish the mysterious path of something as complex, difficult, and almost impossible as capturing beauty in a moment, suspending it in time, and framing it for eternity’.
Miquel Alzueta

What abstract art means to me

Group exhibition Alzueta Gallery
Séneca

January 2024

Jin Angdoo, Aythamy Armas, Manolo Ballesteros, Lawrence Calver, Enrich. R, Daniel Jensen, Scott Licznerski, Guillermo Pfaff, Kes Richardson, Stan Van Steendam, Claudia Valsells

The exhibition delves into the formal strategies of the participating artists, combining a variety of approaches to abstraction.

Although in the early years of the twentieth century, pictorial abstraction was inevitably linked to a spiritual sense of searching for the invisible and the essence of nature that escapes the formal, today, beyond the dematerialization of the artistic object, abstraction is an exercise of perception that generates constant formal challenges. The search for the perfect plastic gesture, the connection with the world of the formal through the material, or the conscious manipulation of pictorial matter. In the center of all of this: Doubt. Doubt as a fundamental part of the process, as a starting point of any creative exercise. The doubt that falls on the formal strategies, the techniques, the discourses. Abstract painting is at the same time a finished work and an open reflection; there is no abstraction without understanding the experience of the artist who seeks to expand the field of visual perception. What to paint gives way to how to paint, and so often is in this pictorial process where artists find the starting point and the end of the road.

Perhaps that is why abstract painting has been described as painting for painters. A form of expression that is not easy to access due to its component of artistic research that starts from reflection and knowledge of one’s own practice. Starting from nothing and beginning to build the painting. But just as abstract painting is created in an active way, this is how it should be observed. To abstract oneself is to move away from the reality of the body, let be carried away by the gaze, create rhythms between the figures, connect the different parts, accept the formal ambiguity and the chromatism as an articulating component, and understand that the material contains its own language.
Text by Carolina-Laia Puigdevall

La Terre est bleu comme un orange

Group exhibition Alzueta Gallery,
Madrid

November 2023

Cesc Abad, Hugo Alonso, Manolo Ballesteros, Sune Christiansen, Enrich R., Nacho Eterno, Sabine Finkenauer, Jon Koko, David Macho, Jan Monclús, Guillem Nadal, Juan Narowé, Maru Quiñonero, Kes Richardson, James Rielly, Xevi Solà, Mike Swaney, Guim Tió, Claudia Valsells, Maria Yelletisch

In this exhibition we would like to look at works on paper from that perspective: as a blank surface with apparently no laws, which lead us to the title of this exhibition: ‘La Terre est bleue comme une orange’, in english ‘the earth is blue like an orange’.

Paul Éluard, plays with words in a surrealistic exercise of conveying the absurd, writing illogical phrases in a game of fluent ideas without any apparent logic. Under the surrealist poetry all is possible and everything is admissible.

We have invited a group of artists to immerse themselves in this same task, of creating an artwork as if they were playing, to create what doesn’t look for logic or reason, and that, without any pretension, shows itself for what it is: a preliminary idea, a masterpiece, a provocation. Whatever that artist feels this work should be at this moment in time.

Paper is not only what you think it is, it is not just a surface, paper is the work itself, paper is that preliminary moment that holds all possibilities. As abstract as it might be, paper holds the idea of the earth being blue as an orange. Paper holds the most honest and playful topics without questioning their rationality, without putting yet to test the possibilities of an Idea, paper is, over all, a safe space for playing and finding a discourse.

Siete Sillas 8

an AOO EXERCISE IN ‘COLORING’
WITH CLAUDIA VALSELLS

2020

A project with AOO where I created and designed the different colour combinations for ‘8 Chair’.

The 8 Chair is the result of an AOO exercise where flat sheets of plywood are bent, under pressure and in a simple way, using different structures as support, achieving instantaneously and without moulds to generate what ends up being seats and backs. The resulting piece is formed by two frames joined together and stripped of any decorative element that, together with the backrest and seat, generate a comfortable, lightweight and plastically unique chair.

To enhance its visual uniqueness, the artist Claudia Valsells has developed a series of colour combinations that denote her exploration of beauty. The chair transmits us the tactility of the work through the palette; because what can be touched is tangible and brings us closer to its reality. Each chair is painted in more than one color to emphasize the meaning of each one when it enters in contrast with the others.

This proposal is the result of a collaboration that normally does not occur in the world of art and design, is a dialogue between disciplines that converge in a common and unlikely end point for each of the participants and also an excuse between two professionals that ends with a good friendship.

If we had to design a chair we would talk about the conceptual and formal process of an object. If we had to talk about colour we would talk about texture and paint, about tones and shades. And here we talk about adding two languages and a single idea: a series of chairs shaped in a plastic exercise and the chromatic development that enhances, perhaps, their uniqueness. The object is raised piece by piece and is given a different value: the sense is decontextualized and a new proposal grows.

Claudia is an artist specialized in color. A multidisciplinary creator, she discovered painting through her own instinct for color. Her formation as a mural painter gave her a professional opportunity, and her passion in color her own artistic language.

Based on her reflections as an artist, her experience in color prediction and mural painting, brings to all her work, with color as a primary tool, an identifiable language.

She began her artistic career painting and soon realized that she had a natural ability with color. In her own words, ‘All I have done throughout my career has been to cultivate this natural gift. First as a mural painter, then designing color charts and prescribing color and finally as an artist’.

The tireless search for the reason of color and its form has obsessed artists throughout history, from the very creation of pictorial expression. Claudia Valsells knows about this and she aims to portray this into her artwork.

In her beginnings as a colorist she worked on commissions, for architects, and on projects of different scales advising and prescribing color. Soon she founded her own Studio, under the name of ‘Arts&Claus’, in allegory to the ‘Arts&Craft’ movement where she always found a lot of inspiration. She specialized in mural painting, color applied to architecture, and interior design for 20 years on projects in Spain and abroad. Her role was to convey the value of color and its texture in the decoration of interior spaces working closely with professionals in the fields of art, design, and architecture. It was in this context, working close with architects and designers, that she found out the communication between professionals was based by the painting companies, relegated the work of the colorist to the background due to ignorance or fear clients to make a mistake or be branded as eccentric. It was then when Claudia Valsells created a specific chromatic selection, a COLOUR CHART, with more than 200 references, which have been used as color communication tools by Spain’s best architects.

Her work has two very defined characteristics: on the one hand her chromatic instinct, a gift of colors and on the other her ability to select, group and interact colors creating a chromatic language of her own.

Currently she combines her activity as an artist with colour prescription in the areas of design and architecture.

Her work is represented, in Spain and abroad, by the Barcelona gallery owner Miquel Alzueta. ALZUETA GALLERY
