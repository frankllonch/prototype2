Moll de l’Ós

8 rue des Beaux-Arts, Paris

From May 27 to June 26 2026

Alzueta Gallery Paris is pleased to present Moll de l’os, an exhibition of new works by Claudia Valsells. The opening will take place on 30 May, and the exhibition will be on view at 8 rue des Beaux-Arts, in the heart of Saint-Germain-des-Prés.

“Moll de l’os brings together a body of paintings made throughout 2026 that investigate the capacity of color to hold experience. With no predetermined subject matter and no representational intent, the works emerge as records of a process sustained over time — one in which painting has become a space of attention and transformation. In these mid-scale formats, the pictorial matter concentrates gesture, silence and presence, inviting the viewer into a direct encounter with what is essential. These are emotional registers, sediments of a lived process. For me, painting is a way of drawing closer to the core — to the marrow of each piece. It is how I test the possibility of continuing to paint, even when it is difficult.

With Moll de l’os, I point to the place where artistic practice confronts its own nucleus — where colour does not explain: it insists.

My hope is that the viewer, too, might connect with their own marrow, and find a personal experience that reaches beyond a purely visual reading.”

Claudia Valsells

Transitions

Alzueta Gallery, Barcelona

September 2025

Transitions is a reflection of the constant duality between the creative process and the finished work. In essence, the work itself becomes a field of oscillation, and it is within this oscillation that it finds its meaning and purpose.

The colors, carefully interwoven , interact with white, gradually seeking juxtaposition. It is within this juxtaposition that we find the transition, which in this context can be understood as a change, a movement, a flow of energy, a recycling of the same. This transition is not limited solely to a formal change but transcends to a deeper level where the works resonate with each other, creating a visual symphony that evolves as we move through the space, crossing invisible lines of connection that keep the canvases in constant conversation.

This exhibition marks a transition from the initial chromatic verticality to a broader and more diverse palette; genuine and light. It is a testimony to how I try to evolved over time and a testament to this quest for new horizons.

This time, under a crucial motto: the work is the path to color. Color exists before the work takes shape and gradually reveals itself as we progress in the creative process. The work is built from the inside out, as an ever-developing process enriched with every brushstroke and every decision made in the studio.

I am not just talking about a mixture of colors and forms. It is a source of energy, a manifestation of the deep connection.

To rethinking,

oscillating between the process of the artwork

and the artwork itself.__

As a field of oscillation,

without oscillation, there is no artwork.

Its process and its culmination.

Like the reality that surrounds us,

ever-changing as well.

The intertwined colors

interact with white,

seeking juxtaposition,

the transition,

understood as a change,

understood as a movement.

Transition from chromatic verticality to the color

palette,

always expanding.

The artwork is not the color,

the artwork is a path to color.

Color exists before the artwork does,

the artwork will come with time

built from the inside out.

The artwork, as something more, is ENERGY.

Claudia Valsells

Mira’m no deixis de mirar-me

Palau de Casavells

From April to June 2025

“Mira’m, no deixis de mirar-me” “Look at Me, Don’t Stop Looking at Me”invites us to explore the work and career of Claudia Valsells, where color is both a physical substance and an intangible emotion. Throughout a life devoted to researching and experimenting with color and the creation of her own palette, the artist has created a language to channel her experience and perception of the world. In her work, color goes beyond aesthetics to become a meaningful, almost linguistic, element.

For the artist, materializing a color is a complex process in three acts: first, she envisions it; then, she creates it through a unique combination of pigments; and finally, she applies it to the canvas, where it engages in dialogue with other colors. Valsells builds on technical knowledge but also surrenders to intuition, finding in painting a space of refuge and communication—both with herself and with the viewer. Each piece, each chromatic interaction, emerges from a delicate interplay of prior knowledge, instinct, and the artist’s lived experience.

The exhibition brings together, for the first time, the series What’s Color?, Color Dialogues, The Joy of Painting, Transitions, and her most recent work, Mapa, in which Valsells explores chromatic juxtaposition with a newfound maturity. This collection of works showcases the evolution of her practice while highlighting the importance of the viewer’s gaze in art. The title, “Mira’m, no deixis de mirar-me”, refers, on one hand, to the act of exposing oneself to public recognition and, on the other, to the essential need to open up to others, not only in the artistic context but in all aspects of life.

The concepts of the eye and the gaze have often been understood as symbols of otherness. In this sense, Sartre asserted that it is through the gaze of others that we recognize ourselves. This dimension of the other is what constitutes and defines us, allowing us to exist as subjects. According to the French philosopher, it is also what humanizes us and, therefore, enables communication. Daily experiences and interactions with others shape multiple identities: we are not made of a single piece.

Valsells’ work becomes a space for connection, where the viewer and the artwork meet through the experience of color. In this exchange, the work finds meaning, and we, as viewers, also uncover something about ourselves. “Mira’m, no deixis de mirar-me” is an open invitation to this silent dialogue, where color becomes a language, speaking to us about who we are, about others, and about the artist’s own journey.

Color dialogues

Alzueta Gallery, Madrid

From December to January 2022

After my last solo exhibition a year ago at Alzueta Gallery Barcelona questioning the meaning of color and introducing my new line of work on canvas, this new exhibition continues the intense meditation I have over color.

Investigating its meaning and the different concepts that poets, thinkers, architects and interior designers have about the meaning of color, my paintings are juxtaposed dialogues on the concept and importance of color as a universal visual emotion, capable of reaching and inspiring every human being.

«The verticality of what starts and doesn’t end. It represents us. It calls us.
It raises a question -what can you build with me?- but, at the same time, also gives us the answer -you are the color that you choose to build yourself with-. The relation between the horizontality of different colors -different concepts, individualities, times, spaces, ideas, islands, houses, worlds, feelings-, uninterrupted colors -life- in its broken forms, supported by the white, that is all colors, and at the same time, none.
Contrast and meaning. Context.»© Flavia Company

What’s color (II)

Espacio Pavilon, Madrid

December 2021

Exhibition curators: Julia Ruiz-Cabello and Gonzalo de Benito.

«The memory of one of my architecture teachers taking off one of his reddish-brown English shoes, rolling up the tour of his pants to show the navy blue color of his socks, and cutting out a piece of clothing from the striped Oxford shirt. All this spectacle in the middle of a construction site to show respectively the color of the wood, the color of the carpet and the different shades of the walls, is an image that I remember with some nostalgia, but that cannot be repeated today.»

Text commissioned by the architect Cristian Cirici on the occasion of my my first color chart self edited in 2000.

«From the scientific experiment to the artistic concern, from Munsell’s diagrams and Goethe’s studies to Le Corbusier’s Salubra and the almost infinite possible combinations of electronics, the color chart has always existed to facilitate the communication of color. A mere organic, hierarchical and chromatic division in its origin, a method of plastic expression and aesthetic positioning in its development, this instrument, which came to democratize design and encourage the creative impulse, has seen in its years of life how the use of color by the architectural sector has been diminishing, limited perhaps because of certain social prejudices and canons encouraged by advertising and the market. The pristine white, the sad black, the nondescript gray, the garish red.

Monopolized the business of colors available by the painting companies, relegated the work of the colorist to the background due to ignorance or fear of architects and clients to make a mistake or be branded as eccentric; it is in this context that the artist Claudia Valsells had an idea: to create a specific chromatic selection for this sector.

In this exhibition we wanted to highlight the work of the colorist, the process of selecting and prescribing colors for a project and the color chart as a tool that facilitates and enables communication between architects and designers. For this reason we have compiled in the exhibition a series of work tools that Claudia, an artist linked to color since her beginnings as a mural painter, used for the elaboration of her own chart: notebooks, mixers, spatulas, annotations, paint covers. It is therefore a retrospective, documentary and personal exhibition, where experiences, stories and testimonies are mixed.

The result of more than twenty years of work by the artist, collaborating with architects and designers in the definition of colors, this color chart is composed of 142 colors applied with real paint, which allows to see the exact reproduction of the color as it will look once applied on the surface. This range of colors, developed for use in architecture, both exterior and interior, allows the colorist to demonstrate Valsells’ natural ability and chromatic instinct, the knowledge of the language that color implies.

This color chart, and the work of its author, Claudia Valsells, is an invitation to explore, to play, and to dare to prescribe colors. Any color from this palette can be achieved, and it is worth the effort to see exactly what one is looking for. We want to reopen the dialogue on the use of color in architecture, to offer one more tool, among many others, that encourages that return to color, with knowledge, dialogue and vision.»

What’s color (I)

Alzueta Gallery, Barcelona

December 2020

Throughout the lockdown I was away from my studio and my art form – painting.

I found great comfort reading pieces by philosophers, historians, thinkers, writers, and other artists. They helped me better understand humanity and its role in this globalized world.

During the past months I have invited them to consider and answer the question: ‘What does color mean to you?’

First of all, to open up a dialogue, to grasp new ways of seeing colors, other perspectives, but also to better understand the world. I wanted to think of color in a broader context, outside of the artistic view, and to discover the notion of color as it is thought of by others.

Now, this whole journey has become a process of identifying and synthesizing knowledge, when possible, into color, with the wish to create and share a ‘Color Glossary’ where color meets perception. For what purpose?

Because To me, COLOR is a universal visual emotion, capable of reaching and inspiring every human being.

Claudia Valsells

La terre est bleu comme un organge

Alzueta Gallery, Madrid

November 2023

Claudia is an artist specialized in color. A multidisciplinary creator, she discovered painting through her own instinct for color. Her formation as a mural painter gave her a professional opportunity, and her passion in color her own artistic language.

Based on her reflections as an artist, her experience in color prediction and mural painting, brings to all her work, with color as a primary tool, an identifiable language.

She began her artistic career painting and soon realized that she had a natural ability with color. In her own words, ‘All I have done throughout my career has been to cultivate this natural gift. First as a mural painter, then designing color charts and prescribing color and finally as an artist’.

The tireless search for the reason of color and its form has obsessed artists throughout history, from the very creation of pictorial expression. Claudia Valsells knows about this and she aims to portray this into her artwork.

In her beginnings as a colorist she worked on commissions, for architects, and on projects of different scales advising and prescribing color. Soon she founded her own Studio, under the name of ‘Arts&Claus’, in allegory to the ‘Arts&Craft’ movement where she always found a lot of inspiration. She specialized in mural painting, color applied to architecture, and interior design for 20 years on projects in Spain and abroad. Her role was to convey the value of color and its texture in the decoration of interior spaces working closely with professionals in the fields of art, design, and architecture. It was in this context, working close with architects and designers, that she found out the communication between professionals was based by the painting companies, relegated the work of the colorist to the background due to ignorance or fear clients to make a mistake or be branded as eccentric. It was then when Claudia Valsells created a specific chromatic selection, a COLOUR CHART, with more than 200 references, which have been used as color communication tools by Spain’s best architects.

Her work has two very defined characteristics: on the one hand her chromatic instinct, a gift of colors and on the other her ability to select, group and interact colors creating a chromatic language of her own.
